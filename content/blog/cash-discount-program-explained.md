If you take cards, processing is one of the few costs that scales with how well
you do. Sell more, pay more. A **cash discount program** is one of the two ways
to deal with that, and it is the one most owners have heard of without ever
getting a straight explanation of how it actually works at the counter.

Here is the plain version, and what to check before you move.

## What a cash discount program actually does

Under a normal setup, you pay a percentage of every card sale to the processor.
That cost comes out of your margin. Under a cash discount program, the
processing cost moves onto the transaction itself, so the customer paying by
card covers it rather than you absorbing it.

At EPAY we call ours the **Consumer Choice Program**. The effect on your side is
simple: your processing rate is 0%. You keep 100% of the sale.

The name is worth pausing on. "Consumer choice" is the accurate description of
what happens — the customer sees both prices and chooses which one they want to
pay. Cash is cheaper. Card costs a little more. Nobody is surprised at the
register, because the difference is shown before they decide.

## What it looks like at the counter

This is the part owners care about, and it is less dramatic than people expect.

Your point of sale shows two prices: one for cash, one for card. A customer
paying cash pays the lower one. A customer paying by card pays the card price,
which includes the processing cost. The receipt reflects whichever they chose.

That is dual pricing. It is not a surcharge bolted on at the end of the
transaction, and it is not a surprise line item. Both numbers are visible
up front.

Whether you change your shelf prices to match is a separate decision. Our
program is compliant with or without shelf price changes, per your state's
requirements — some owners reprice everything, others leave the shelf alone and
let the register show both figures.

## Cash discount versus flat rate

Consumer Choice is not the only option, and it is not automatically the right
one. The alternative is a straightforward flat rate: **2.3% + 5¢** on every
card, with no tiered pricing and no surprises buried in the statement.

Which one fits depends on things only you know:

- **Your ticket size.** The per-transaction cost matters more on small tickets
  than on large ones.
- **Your customer mix.** A business where most people already pay cash feels a
  cash discount program differently than one where nearly every sale is a card.
- **How you want to be seen.** Some owners are comfortable showing two prices.
  Others would rather quote one number and absorb the cost. Both are legitimate.

## What to check before you switch

Three things, in order.

**Read your current statement first.** Not the summary box on the front page —
the line items on the back. Most owners are surprised by what is in there, and
you cannot tell whether a change helps until you know what you are actually
paying today. We will break a statement down line by line if you send one over;
it takes about two minutes to upload.

**Confirm what applies where you are.** Rules around cash discounting, dual
pricing and surcharging vary by state and change over time, and card network
rules sit on top of that. We are not lawyers and this is not legal advice — ask
someone who can advise on your specific situation before you commit.

qualifying merchants get POS hardware placed with no upfront cost — no lease and
